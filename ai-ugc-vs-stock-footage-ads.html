Honest Comparison

Stock footage or AI video ads for your next campaign

This page compares building a vertical ad from licensed stock footage against commissioning AI-generated video from us. We sell the AI side, and we are saying that up front rather than hiding it. What follows is honest, including the cases where stock footage is plainly the better buy and we are not.

We sell one of these two things. So we have written down exactly where the other one wins.

The short answer

Use stock footage when the ad sells a mood, a place or a lifestyle and never needs to show your actual product on screen, because that is where stock is fast, real and cheap. Use us when the ad has to show the product you sell and have a presenter say something specific about it, because a stock library holds footage of everything except the thing you are selling.

Where this comparison stops flattering us

The example above prices an editor. Many brands already have one. If someone on your team or your retainer cuts video anyway, an unlimited subscription makes the raw material near-free and the marginal cost of your next ad is time you are already paying for. Even hired out, the bottom of the published range for a short-form edit is $25–$75 — below our per-ad price in any pack. So “AI is cheaper than stock” is not true across the board. It is true when the editing labour lands on the invoice, and false when it is already on payroll. If you have an editor, the honest question is not price at all: it is whether the ad needs your product and your words on screen.

When stock footage is the better buy

Mood over product

When the ad sells atmosphere, scenery or a lifestyle and never needs your product in close-up, real filmed footage carries it better than anything generated.

Real places, real weather

Cities, crowds, weather, coastline and nature shot by a real crew still read as true in ways synthetic footage cannot match, so for these scenes start there.

You have an editor

If a brand already employs an editor, an unlimited subscription makes raw footage close to free, so the marginal cost of the next ad is mainly that person's time.

No AI disclosure

If the platform or the client requires footage with no synthetic origin and no disclosure label, real licensed stock is simply the only honest option on the table.

When AI production is the better buy

The product must show

When the ad must show your actual product, we build shots from your photographs, so viewers see the thing you sell rather than a generic stand-in.

Someone must say it

When a specific claim or hook has to come out of a presenter's mouth, lip-synced to camera, stock cannot help, because nobody in a library is speaking your script.

Cheap hook testing

Trying several openings against each other makes a fresh hook a re-render for us, not a fresh shoot or re-cut, so testing the openings costs you little.

Not in any library

Nothing on screen is licensable by a competitor, because the shots are built from your product and your script, so no rival can run the same ad as you.

Every frame we deliver is AI-generated and disclosed as such where the ad runs, and we think you should disclose too. Disclosure is not the disadvantage people assume, because major ad platforms now expect and accommodate it.
